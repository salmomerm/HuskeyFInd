HuskyFind — UW Campus Lost & Found Platform

A UW-specific digital lost & found platform designed to solve a problem nearly every student runs into: losing something on a huge campus with no central, searchable way to get it back. Built as a 2-person team project (with Omar) for an INFO 360 user research and design course, from initial research through a tested high-fidelity prototype.

The problem

We interviewed UW students, faculty, and staff who'd lost or found something on campus. The pattern was consistent: items get posted in scattered group chats or left at a random building's front desk with no follow-up, and most people give up searching within 24–48 hours and just pay to replace what they lost. UW has dozens of separate, uncoordinated lost & found desks and no digital, campus-wide way to search or post.

How might we help UW students and faculty easily post and search for lost items on campus so they can recover their belongings quickly, reduce the financial and emotional burden of losing essential items, and feel supported by a trustworthy campus community?

What I did
Conducted and synthesized user interviews (UW students, faculty/staff, and people who'd run similar platforms elsewhere) into an empathy map and affinity map of the key pain points
Built two personas — a student who regularly loses items, and front-desk staff managing lost property with no shared system — to ground design decisions in real stakeholder needs
Designed and iterated the UX from low-fidelity to high-fidelity prototype, including the login, home, search, post, and item-detail flows
Ran Wizard of Oz and think-aloud usability testing on two core scenarios (searching for a lost Husky Card, posting a found backpack), then redesigned based on the results — adding a "Message Finder" flow and a claimed-item status after testing showed users couldn't tell how to contact a finder, and adding an AI privacy scanner plus a confirmation screen after testing showed users were unsure how to safely post sensitive items
Scoped and proposed the AI feature: a natural-language chat assistant that lets a user describe a lost item in plain language and surfaces matching posts, plus an AI privacy scan that automatically blurs sensitive info (like a Husky Card number) in photos before they're posted
Co-presented the final case study and prototype walkthrough
Key features

Lost/found posting with photos, category- and building-based search, in-app messaging (no personal contact info required), an AI privacy scan for sensitive photos, and claimed-item status tracking.

Tools

Figma (prototyping) · FigJam (research & process) · Figma Make (AI-assisted high-fidelity prototype)
